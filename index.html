How a domain expires

A domain doesn't become free the moment it expires. Most go through these stages (exact days depend on the ending and registrar):

ActivePaid up
Grace0–45 days after expiry. Owner renews at normal price
Redemption~30 days. Owner can still recover it (costly)
Pending delete5 days. No one can touch it
AvailableAnyone can register

So a domain usually becomes free about 75–80 days after its expiry date, and only if the owner never renews. Most owners do renew; many domains also have auto-renew turned on.

Finding domains worth buying

  1. Add domains you like to the watchlist or the daily scan.
  2. Watch for Expired and Redemption. These may drop soon.
  3. When one shows Available, register it at any registrar (links appear on the card).
  4. For in-demand names, backorder services (registrar "backorder" or "drop catch") try to grab it the second it drops.
  5. Prefer short, easy-to-say, older .com / .in names without hyphens or numbers. The card gives a quick score.

Play it fair (and stay out of trouble)

  • Don't buy a business's name or brand to sell it back to them. Under the domain dispute rules (UDRP, and INDRP for .in), that's "bad-faith registration". The owner can take the domain back through a complaint, and you lose what you paid.
  • Safe ways to earn: buy generic dropped names (words, places, topics) and list them on marketplaces like Sedo, Afternic or Dan.com; or build small sites on them.
  • Even better recurring income: offer website owners a renewal reminder or management service, so you're paid every year for keeping their domain safe. The heads-up message button on each card drafts that message for you.

Where the data comes from

Lookups use RDAP, the official registry service that replaced WHOIS. It's run by the registries themselves, is free, and needs no account. Some country endings don't publish RDAP yet; those show a note instead of a date.
